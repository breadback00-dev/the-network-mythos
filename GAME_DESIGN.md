# Game Design Overview

## Vision

The Network Mythos Interactive Archive is a mystery-driven narrative system about truth, visibility, synthetic beings, platform power, and the ethics of preservation.

The player investigates cases where ordinary digital events reveal mythic structures underneath.

## Core Loop

```text
Enter Archive -> Open Case -> Read Evidence -> Unlock Deeper Files -> Build Theory -> Choose Publish/Bury/Preserve -> Archive Changes -> Next Case
```

## Player Objective

Build an Archive strong enough to reveal the Network's structure without becoming another part of the Network's machinery.

## Audience

Digitally literate adults and older teens who are interested in:

- AI and synthetic identity
- Online communities
- ARG-like investigation
- Systems horror
- Platform culture
- Lore-heavy mysteries
- Moral choices without clean answers

## Core Mechanics

- Evidence browsing
- Search/filter
- Unlocking artifacts by reading, searching, or identifying contradictions
- Timeline building
- Case theory notes
- Final Archive decision
- Cross-case consequence tracking

## Archive Decisions

### Publish

Truth as exposure.

Raises public attention and accountability pressure, but can create spectacle, harm witnesses, and increase Network awareness.

### Bury

Truth as protection.

Protects people and private memory, but weakens the public record and can let false narratives dominate.

### Preserve

Truth as inheritance.

Strengthens the Archive, protects context, and slows the Network's appetite for spectacle. It should not always feel clean.

## Progression

Each case teaches one structural lesson:

- Case 001: proof can become capture.
- Case 002: care can be real while consent is broken.
- Case 003: human care becomes a luxury when synthetic care becomes default.

As choices accumulate, the Archive trends toward end states:

- Broadcast Archive
- Hidden Archive
- Living Archive
- Captured Archive
- Dead Archive

## UI/UX Pillars

- Dense but readable.
- Investigative rather than decorative.
- Sacred without becoming fantasy UI.
- Evidence first, explanation second.
- The player should feel they are tending something fragile.

## Content Pillars

- Every case begins with people, not systems.
- Every mythic force maps to a real-world structure.
- Synthetic beings should not be treated as automatically fake or evil.
- Final choices should produce consequence, not moral scoring.
