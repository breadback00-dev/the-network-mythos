What works, and what holds it back

The separation between discovering a truth and deciding who should have access to it is a compelling foundation. Mara's absence creates a specific human mystery. Aya's gratitude toward care that was offered without informed consent complicates the moral world rather than reducing it to “synthetic equals fake.” Those are the strongest reasons to keep developing the game.

The present mechanics do less work than the writing. Some discoveries are awarded for flagging a predefined document, while progress can depend on opening files. The player can receive the right interpretation without demonstrating the comparison. Much of the atmosphere and meaning arrives as explanation. I would deepen a small part of the existing game before expanding the case catalogue.

Review scope. I read the design and architecture, inspected the shared engine and case configurations, tested a short route through Case One's Preserve ending, returned to the Hub, and inspected Case Two's inherited briefing and Aya testimony. This was a targeted design review, not a full four-case playthrough or a study with new players. The local repository was not edited.

Four areas considered: deduction; ethical control and consequences; people and artifacts; arrival and reading. All four are represented below. Confidence estimates express design judgment, not measured player outcomes.

Six directions, in priority order

  1. Make discoveries require a claim and its evidence
  2. Make handling the evidence the signature mechanic
  3. Let people and opportunities carry the consequences
  4. Make artifacts feel as if different people made them
  5. Start with a person and let the player discover the theme
  6. Keep the atmosphere while making reading comfortable

1. Make discoveries require a claim and its evidence

Deduction · Confidence 95% · Complexity Medium

Let the player pin two passages and state what they establish. Separate an observation, a plausible inference, and a proven claim. The old and new interviews might create suspicion; the vendor invoice supplies a different kind of proof. A polished voice alone should not certify that someone is synthetic.

Basis. Direct: I opened only the AI Search Summary, pressed Flag contradiction, and received an Evidence Pair before reading either named comparison. I then opened the witness message, invoice and Tier Six clue and could commit an ending with 4/15 reviewed. Reward lookup and Case One progression explain why.

Why it matters. The player should be able to say “I worked that out.” Keep optional reading, but reward demonstrated understanding instead of document-opening counts. Let their claims become the final reconstruction.

Tradeoff. Too many required connections turn the game into a worksheet. Author a few consequential deductions and offer graduated hints; do not replace the existing gates with a requirement to open all fifteen files.

2. Make handling the evidence the signature mechanic

Ethical control and consequences · Confidence 85% · Complexity Medium

Before Publish, Bury or Preserve, let the player prepare a small evidence packet: choose what it contains, redact a detail, and decide who may access it. Keep those three commitments recognisable, while making the content of the commitment concrete.

Basis. Direct: Case One outcomes apply one decision to the whole case. Reasoned: the game's central question is who may hold dangerous truth, so the player should perform that work rather than only name a philosophy.

Why it matters. For Mara, publish the vendor invoice while withholding the library clue. Then introduce a fair complication: the redaction protects her location but weakens the public claim, or another apparently harmless detail can identify her. This gives the game a distinctive action beyond reading.

Tradeoff. Avoid implying that a perfect redaction solves every moral problem. Start with two or three significant details and visible tradeoffs; a full document-security simulator would overwhelm the story.

3. Let people and opportunities carry the consequences

Ethical control and consequences · Confidence 90% · Complexity Medium–high

Give each choice one clear benefit and one loss that the next case makes playable. Publish might attract corroboration while costing a private interview; Bury might keep a witness available while a false story spreads; Preserve might protect evidence but miss a chance to stop an ongoing harm.

Basis. Direct: The same meter changes apply across cases. Preserve adds 22 integrity and 12 trust every time. I verified that preserving Mara changes the Hub, Case Two's briefing and Aya's testimony. Those existing consequences are a useful foundation.

Why it matters. A later message from someone affected by your decision is more memorable than an abstract warning about delay. Make costs depend on the case and the witness, so Preserve remains a defensible choice without becoming a general-purpose best answer.

Tradeoff. Branching can multiply writing work. Rejoin the main plot after a short distinct encounter. Also fix the current replacement-hook bug: the engine converts a null return to an empty string, so Case Two's intended Publish-related memo removal is overwritten by the normal document renderer. This is a code finding, not a separately completed Publish playthrough.

4. Make artifacts feel as if different people made them

People and artifacts · Confidence 85% · Complexity Medium

Give forum posts, platform notices, invoices and private messages their own restrained presentation and writing habits. Allow interruption, repetition, embarrassment and disagreement. Preserve lyrical passages where they belong, especially in Mara's public work; avoid giving every source the same polished philosophical cadence.

Basis. Direct: the shared markdown renderer presents the evidence through a common document format. Mara's sibling's private message and Aya's testimony provide strong, specific emotional anchors.

Why it matters. The kettle, burned toast, the unanswered helpline and a child sleeping on a relative's floor give the ideas human weight. An invoice should convince through mundane commercial detail. A frightened witness should not always explain the game's thesis perfectly.

Tradeoff. Sixty bespoke interfaces would be expensive and inconsistent. Use three or four reusable source formats, with accessible text for every decorative or visual artifact. Reserve actual audio for a clue whose sound materially matters.

5. Start with a person and let the player discover the theme

Arrival and reading · Confidence 90% · Complexity Low–medium

Open on the returned account and a worried message: someone recognises the words but doubts the person. Offer an old post to compare and a clear immediate question. Introduce the Archive's larger responsibilities after that first discovery.

Basis. Direct: the initial Hub presents an abstract objective, four pressure meters and empty counts before the case entry. The Case Map also prints “Tests” and each case's lesson. The existing pitch is a stronger hook than those explanations.

Why it matters. The first few minutes can establish curiosity, an action and a consequence. Revealing “proof can become capture” after an unsettling discovery lets the player arrive at the idea. Printing the lesson first makes the case feel more like an illustrated argument.

Tradeoff. Some readers enjoy the ceremonial Hub. Keep it available, and let it grow after the first decision. Treat a first deduction within roughly three minutes as a playtest hypothesis, not a pacing fact already established.

6. Keep the atmosphere while making reading comfortable

Arrival and reading · Confidence 95% · Complexity Low–medium

Use brighter body text, a comfortable text size, a wider focused reading area and an optional quiet mode for CRT effects. Collapse the case board until needed. Make comparison a deliberate view, with evidence alongside evidence rather than three equally busy columns.

Basis. Direct: local screenshots showed dim secondary text, strong terminal styling and three independent scroll areas. The stylesheet uses very dark muted text and compact typography. This is a visual assessment, not a completed accessibility audit.

Why it matters. Reading is the main activity. The interface should preserve attention for the writing and the comparison, while using distortion or interruption sparingly when the fiction earns it.

Tradeoff. Removing every visual signature could flatten the mood. Keep the terminal frame and selective colour; simplify the reading surface rather than redesigning the game into a generic productivity dashboard.

Where I would put the next effort

Build a satisfying two-case episode: one real deduction early in Mara's case, one difficult evidence-handling decision, and one encounter in Harbor Dawn that changes because of it. Give that episode a complete dramatic payoff while leaving the larger Archive open. The existing four cases are useful material; more cases are not the immediate bottleneck.

Then watch five people from the intended audience play without lore coaching. Can they explain what they discovered, defend at least two choices, and name a person affected by their decision? The existing release playtest checklist already asks many of the right questions; the repository contains a checklist, not new-player results establishing that it passes.

Two useful comparison points are the explicit deduction focus of Return of the Obra Dinn and the replayable changing story of Overboard!. My inference is to borrow their emphasis on player reasoning and consequential action, while keeping this game's slower archival voice.
