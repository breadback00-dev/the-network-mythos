# 02: Blackwater Floods Summary

## Metadata

- Source type: crisis summary
- Compiled by: Civic Memory Project
- Event window: 29 August-4 September 2028
- Region: Blackwater Estuary
- Status: public archive excerpt

## Artifact

The Blackwater Floods began after three days of stalled rain met a failed tide barrier and an overloaded drainage system. The first public warning described the event as "localized surface flooding."

By the second night, evacuation routes were underwater.

By the third morning, the emergency call system had collapsed under volume. Residents used group chats, school mailing lists, delivery apps, church pages, neighborhood forums, and short-range radio to coordinate rescue and supplies.

Official casualty numbers remain disputed. The confirmed count is 118 dead, 340 injured, and more than 18,000 displaced. Local organizers claim the number of uncounted deaths is higher, especially among undocumented workers, elderly residents, and people living in informal housing near the estuary.

Harbor Dawn formed six days after the first flood warning. It began as a logistics board and became a grief-support forum within two weeks.

Municipal review later found failures in infrastructure maintenance, emergency communication, disability evacuation planning, and temporary housing coordination.

No criminal charges were filed.

## Investigator Notes

The disaster was physical, local, and infrastructural before it became digital.

Harbor Dawn's value came from coordination failure elsewhere.

The "no blame in support threads" norm may have begun as emotional containment, but later evidence suggests it was also useful to institutions avoiding liability.
