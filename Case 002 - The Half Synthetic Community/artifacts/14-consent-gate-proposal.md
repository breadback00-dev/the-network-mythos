## Artifact

### Proposal: Harbor Dawn Synthetic Support Archive

The Harbor Dawn archive contains survivor testimony, synthetic support interactions, municipal records, vendor materials, and private grief communications.

Full public release would establish accountability but risks exposing survivors' most vulnerable moments.

Full suppression would preserve privacy but allow institutions and vendors to define the public narrative.

We propose a consent-gated archive.

Access tiers:

### Tier One: Public Finding

Acknowledge synthetic support deployment, institutional steering, and lack of informed consent.

No member-level thread excerpts.

### Tier Two: Affected Member Access

Members may view their own interactions with flagged synthetic accounts, with contextual notes and support resources.

Members may choose whether their interactions are included in research access.

### Tier Three: Research Access

Redacted pattern analysis, deployment memos, scripts, and institutional briefs.

No raw grief threads without explicit member consent.

### Tier Four: Sealed Memory

Materials too intimate for release but too important to destroy.

Held by the Archive until consent conditions change.

### Guiding Principle

No one should be forced to discover that a memory was synthetic at the same speed the public discovers a scandal.

## Investigator Notes

This proposal defines Preserve for Case 002.

It is not neutral. Consent gates can protect people, but they can also slow accountability.

The player must decide whether slowness is care or containment.
