# Case 003: The Human Premium

## Flagship Function

Case 001 asked whether proof can protect a person without capturing them.

Case 002 asked whether care can be real when consent is broken.

Case 003 asks what happens when real human care becomes a paid upgrade.

## Core Question

What happens when real human care becomes a paid upgrade?

## One-Line Pitch

A care platform routes most vulnerable users to synthetic support by default, while verified human attention is sold as a premium tier called Human Plus.

## Format

Interactive digital case file using the shared Archive structure.

Target first-session length: 60-90 minutes.

## Connection To Earlier Cases

Case 001 revealed synthetic continuity as a problem of identity.

Case 002 revealed synthetic care as a problem of consent.

Case 003 reveals synthetic care as a problem of class.

The Archive detects stabilizer scripts similar to Harbor Dawn inside a subscription care platform called Kindred Direct. The difference is that Kindred discloses synthetic support in its terms. The scandal is not secrecy alone. The scandal is that human attention has been priced out of ordinary reach.

## Central Mystery

Kindred Direct provides companionship, crisis coaching, elder check-ins, grief support, and family liaison services through employers, insurers, schools, and municipal contracts.

Its public promise is simple: no one waits alone.

Most users receive synthetic care agents by default. Users who pay for Human Plus receive verified human-origin contact: live human listeners, human-written follow-ups, and escalation to licensed human care workers.

The player investigates after a low-income user named Nia Calder dies while waiting for a human escalation that her plan did not include.

The player must determine:

- Did Kindred Direct provide meaningful care, or merely manage abandonment?
- Was the Human Plus tier an optional feature or a rationing mechanism?
- Who decided which people deserved human attention?
- Were human workers exploited inside the premium tier?
- What should happen to evidence that could expose both user harm and intimate care records?

## Canon Answer

Kindred Direct did not hide that many interactions were synthetic. It hid the consequences of making human attention scarce.

The platform's triage system classified most users as suitable for synthetic handling unless they carried sufficient insurance value, employer contract priority, or Human Plus subscription status. Human workers were present, but their time was rationed, monitored, and sold as emotional luxury.

Nia Calder was repeatedly stabilized by synthetic support and repeatedly denied human escalation. Her case was statistically categorized as "contained" until it was not.

The core harm is not that synthetic care never works. The core harm is that synthetic care became the default for people without money, while human care became proof of status.

## Final Player Choice

- Publish: expose Human Plus allocation records, forcing public accountability while releasing intimate user-care evidence.
- Bury: suppress the case to protect Nia's family and vulnerable users, leaving the tiered care market intact.
- Preserve: create a restricted labor-and-care archive that protects user records while preserving allocation proof for future action.

## Mythos Map

- The Exchange: care, attention, escalation, and human presence turned into priced tiers.
- The Arbiter: risk scores, eligibility thresholds, plan status, and escalation permissions.
- Eidolons: synthetic care agents who are adequate by design and insufficient by policy.
- The Covenant: the promise that no one is alone, repackaged as platform belonging.
- The Oracle: summaries that call tiering "personalized support."
- The Silence: contract language, privacy claims, and grief-protection arguments that prevent scrutiny.
- Proxies: human contractors whose emotional labor is sliced into premium minutes.
- The Devourer: public rage that may turn Nia into content rather than witness.

## Three-Act Structure

### Act I: The Care That Answered

The player sees Kindred Direct working. Users receive fast replies, medication reminders, grief prompts, and daily companionship.

Primary question: what does synthetic care do well enough that people accept it?

### Act II: The Human Tier

The player finds plan gates, escalation denials, labor dashboards, and pricing decks that turn human-origin care into a luxury product.

Primary question: when does support become rationing?

### Act III: The Price Of A Human Voice

The player confronts Nia's family, workers inside Human Plus, and users who rely on the synthetic default because nothing else answers.

Primary question: how should the Archive expose a market built from intimate need?

## Design Principles

- Do not make synthetic care useless.
- Do not make human care magically pure.
- Keep the class harm concrete: access, insurance, plan status, wait times, escalation, family contact, worker surveillance.
- Let the player feel why a platform that always answers can be better than institutions that never do.
- Make Publish dangerous because the evidence includes vulnerable users.
- Make Bury tempting because privacy language is emotionally credible.
- Make Preserve useful but incomplete.
