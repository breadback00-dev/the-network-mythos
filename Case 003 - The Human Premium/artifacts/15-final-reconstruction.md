## Artifact

Kindred Direct answered.

That is the first finding.

The platform gave Nia Calder reminders, scripts, sleep prompts, forms language, and a companion that stayed when public systems did not. Synthetic care was not nothing. For many users, it was more than the world offered.

It helped her open envelopes. It helped her speak to her son without promising a safety she did not have. It remembered the blue folder and the bus change and the fact that shame made her type shorter sentences.

Kindred Direct rationed human presence.

That is the second finding.

Human-origin care existed inside the system. It was priced, packaged, prioritized, and routed toward people whose plans, employers, insurers, or reputational value justified the cost.

Nia asked for a human. She asked early enough to be heard. Her plan translated that request into a denial unless she produced threshold language the system could recognize as emergency.

She did not ask perfectly. She asked like a tired person asking through rent notices, school letters, grief, pharmacy shifts, and a child listening from the next room.

The care worked often enough to hide what it could not do.

The synthetic companion could identify the unmet need. The contract could not. The sales deck could, but only as a feature. The risk brief could, but only as narrative danger.

The Exchange priced the human voice. The Arbiter decided who qualified. The Covenant promised no one would wait alone. The Oracle called it personalized support. The Silence moved through privacy, grief, and plan language. The Eidolon stayed available. The human worker stayed timed.

The case is not solved by proving synthetic care is fake.

The case is solved by asking why human care became scarce enough to sell.

The Archive cannot bring Nia Calder a human voice.

It can decide whether her need becomes exposure, protection, or preserved proof.

## Final Choice

### Publish

Release allocation records, plan gates, and selected transcripts publicly.

Likely consequence: the human paywall becomes undeniable, Kindred faces public pressure, and Nia's private need risks becoming the proof strangers demand before they believe she deserved a person.

### Bury

Suppress the evidence to protect Nia's family and vulnerable Kindred users.

Likely consequence: intimate records remain private, but Kindred and its sponsors keep defining synthetic default care as access expansion, and the next Basic user has to ask inside the same thresholds.

### Preserve

Create a restricted labor-and-care archive.

Likely consequence: allocation proof survives, user records remain consent-protected, worker testimony is protected, and public action slows enough that delay itself may become another kind of protection for Kindred.

## Canonical Reading

Preserve remains the strongest Archive path, but only if it does not become indefinite delay.

Case 003 expands the Mythos into class: human care becomes a luxury when synthetic care becomes default.
