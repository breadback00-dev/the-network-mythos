## Artifact

### Proposal: Sealed Inheritance Protocol

The Lost Archive contains records whose original consent routes are damaged, absent, or destroyed.

The Archive cannot treat absence of consent as permission.

The Archive also cannot let institutions use destroyed consent routes to make harm unverifiable.

We propose:

### Proof-Of-Erasure Markers

Publicly acknowledge that a record existed, was deleted or hidden, and left verifiable residue.

Do not publish raw contents by default.

Each marker must include:

- proof type: presence, deletion, harm, content, or residue
- confidence level
- origin of fragment
- current exposure risk
- whether any affected witness has reviewed the marker
- what the marker explicitly does not prove

### Consent Proxy Panels

For deceased or unreachable subjects, access decisions require a panel made of family representatives, affected-community members, worker witnesses, and Archive stewards.

No single institution controls access.

Minimum panel composition:

- one family or kinship proxy where safe and available
- one affected-community representative
- one worker or system-process witness
- one Archive steward
- one rotating dissent reviewer whose role is to argue against opening

### Delayed Access

Some materials may become available after defined conditions:

- family consent
- worker safety
- legal risk expiration
- corroborating evidence
- affected-community review

Delay cannot be indefinite by default. Every sealed fragment receives a review date, a named burden-holder, and a written reason the Archive can later challenge.

### Context Locks

Fragments cannot be separated from uncertainty notes, corruption warnings, or provenance.

If a fragment is exported, quoted, taught from, or summarized, the context lock travels with it.

Breaking a context lock automatically suspends future access for the requester.

### Right To Remain Lost

Some records may be marked as known-erased and never opened publicly.

The Archive preserves the fact of erasure without consuming the person.

This status can be requested by proxies, affected communities, or the Archive itself, but it must be contested on a schedule so "lost" does not become a polite word for abandoned.

### Appeals And Burdens

Families may appeal a refusal to acknowledge presence.

Workers may appeal exposure decisions that increase retaliation risk.

Affected communities may appeal public markers that turn people into lists.

Archive stewards must publish an annual burden ledger:

- markers acknowledged
- raw rooms opened
- raw rooms refused
- appeals granted
- appeals denied
- fragments still sealed because the Archive could not justify either opening or destruction

Failure condition:

If inheritance becomes indefinite secrecy, the protocol has failed.

If inheritance becomes public hunger with slower steps, the protocol has failed.

If the Archive becomes the only place allowed to know, the protocol has failed.

If families must beg forever for proof of presence, the protocol has failed.

## Investigator Notes

It should feel like responsibility, not relief.

The protocol should not read as a cure. It is a machine for making the Archive answerable while it holds things no one can cleanly own.
