## Artifact

### Ilya Sen

You keep coming back to rooms. Rooms, kitchens, porches, noticeboards. For someone writing about platforms, you are very committed to small architecture.

### Mara Vale

Because scale is where lies get comfortable.

I do not mean that small things are automatically honest. Anyone who has lived in a family knows better. Small rooms can be cruel. Local life can be suffocating. I am not selling gingham curtains as resistance.

But scale changes what can be asked of a person. A room can ask you to be present. A platform asks you to be reproducible.

Sorry, the kettle is going.

### [audio gap: 00:22]

### Mara Vale

Right. Where was I?

### Ilya Sen

Reproducible.

### Mara Vale

Yes. That.

Online, the self that survives is the self that can be summoned again. Same tone, same wound, same clarity, same usefulness. It is not that the performance is false. It is that the performance becomes the part the system knows how to find.

And then people love that part. Truly love it. I do not want to sneer at that. The love is real.

But a loved part is still a part.

### Ilya Sen

So what protects the rest?

### Mara Vale

Interruption. Weather. Hunger. Someone knocking. A kettle. A bus being late. The things that make a person unavailable to the version of themselves that can be consumed.

If proof destroys the thing proven, then the proof belongs to the destroyer.

That sounds more polished than I mean it to.

What I mean is: some things stay alive because they are not dragged into the room where everyone can agree they saw them.
