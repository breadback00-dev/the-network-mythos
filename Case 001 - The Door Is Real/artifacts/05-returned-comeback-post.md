## Artifact

Hello, the Porchlight.

I have missed you more than I knew how to say.

Four years ago, I left because I did not know how to remain present without becoming available for use.

I needed silence. I needed distance. I needed to learn whether there was any part of me that did not arrive already shaped for reply.

There was.

But there was also something I did not know how to admit then.

Absence has consequences.

The Network is not outside us anymore. It is how many of us find each other, protect each other, remember each other, and build rooms we could not have built by geography alone. Leaving did not make me pure. It made me unreachable to people I loved.

I am sorry for the harm my silence caused, especially to those who mistook my boundary for abandonment.

I am returning carefully, and not to stand where I stood before.

The answer is not constant presence. It is not disappearance either. It is consent that can be revised, tools that can be refused, and communities that can survive the absence of a single voice.

Over the next few months, I will be restoring selected archive materials, reopening limited Porchlight spaces, and writing about presence without possession.

The door was real.

I no longer think every door opens only one way.

Mara

## Investigator Notes

The post is emotionally effective and widely accepted by the community.

Differences from prior Mara:

- Keeps enough of Mara's old vocabulary to be persuasive.
- Reframes absence as harm without directly denouncing exit.
- Treats reachability as a moral repair.
- Promises selected restoration and reopening, softer than a full relaunch but still contrary to Mara's final-year refusals.
- Converts "the door is real" into a flexible lesson compatible with continued participation.

Earlier drafts of this case treated the post as more obviously wrong. In the final reading, the danger is subtler: the returned account does not sound unlike Mara. It sounds like a version of Mara optimized to soothe the wound her absence created.
